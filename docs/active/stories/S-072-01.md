**Scope:** The desk spends a weekly allowance and has no idea how much of it is
gone. The operator, 2026-08-15, at the moment this became urgent:

> *"we're at the last 35% of our weekly usage and it's halfway through the week
> only."*

That number came from a person looking at a screen. Nothing on this desk can
read it, and nothing changes its behaviour because of it.

## The two ways it goes wrong

The operator named both, and they want different answers:

**1. The session limit is reached and every loop crashes at once.** Four panes
across two machines all discover it simultaneously, mid-ticket, and what is lost
is not the allowance — it is the work in flight. The wanted behaviour is
*"orchestrate low priority lisa loop stoppage, proactively use sonnet 5, tweak
effort per pane"*: **spend the last of it on purpose rather than all at once by
accident.**

**2. A day eats the week.** Wednesday goes well, and Thursday through Sunday are
unavailable. This one cannot be fixed by reacting at the boundary, because by
the time the boundary is visible the week is already gone.

## What this story does not assume

**Not seven even days.** In the operator's words:

> *"we shouldn't aim for a 7-days-a-week constant usage scenario, as sometimes
> frontloading the work and having 4 days free is a valid option."*

So this is **not** a rate limiter that spreads spend evenly. A desk that burns
80% on Monday because Monday was the day to burn it has done nothing wrong. What
it must not do is burn 80% on Monday **without knowing it did**, and discover it
on Thursday.

The distinction is the whole design: *say what has been spent, loudly enough and
early enough that spending it fast is a decision.*

## The measurement already exists

`capture_usage.rs` writes `.lisa/<client>/captures.jsonl` from the Stop hook —
per pane, per turn, with `input_tokens` and `output_tokens`. Every board on this
desk has one. **Nothing reads them.**

Two things are missing before they can be read as a budget:

- **They do not say which model spent the tokens.** `T-071-01-02` is that gap,
  and it is a hard dependency: Sonnet and Opus are not the same price, so an
  unlabelled sum cannot be turned into a fraction of a week.
- **They are per project, per machine.** The desk is four boards on two
  machines. A weekly allowance is one number across all of it, and the
  crossing to gather them is `rail desk --facts`'s problem, already solved.

## Which program does what

The operator asked *"(rail? lisa? steer?)"*. The desk's own invariants answer it:

- **lisa measures and acts.** It writes the captures, it owns the panes, and it
  is the only one that can stop a loop or change what a pane runs. `no-background`
  does not bind it — it is already the thing that runs.
- **rail reports.** A number on the desk line beside the boards. It is
  `read-only-projects` and `no-background`, so it reads and says; it must not
  decide.
- **steer asks.** When there is a choice — *stop the low-priority loops, or ride
  it out* — that is a fork in the inbox, not a rule in a config file.

Anything that puts the deciding in rail, or the measuring in steer, is fighting
what those programs are for.
