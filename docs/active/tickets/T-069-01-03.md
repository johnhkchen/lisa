## Context

**Read `S-069-01` first.**

Soak is currently a rule each machine runs for itself — `DEFAULT_SOAK_HOURS = 24`
in `crates/lisa-cli/src/channel.rs:50`, evaluated against the GitHub release list
every cycle, on every box. That was the only place it could live when the channel
was a local config field.

With channels as packages it belongs to the publisher. **A release lands in canary
immediately and is promoted into nightly a day later if nothing superseded it** —
decided once, where the releases are, rather than re-derived by twenty machines
each holding their own clock and their own network. After that, `brew upgrade` and
`apt-get upgrade` are correct on their own, with no Lisa logic in the path.

## Acceptance Criteria

- **A scheduled job promotes soaked releases**, writing the `lisa-nightly` formula
  (`T-069-01-01`) and the `nightly` apt suite (`T-069-01-02`) to the newest release
  that has cleared the window. It is a schedule, not a release-time step — soak is
  a wait, and nothing at release time can wait for it.
- **"Superseded" is defined and tested.** A tag replaced by a newer one before its
  window closed must not be promoted just because its own clock ran out. Name the
  rule and cover the case where two releases ship inside one window.
- **A promotion that has nothing to do changes nothing** and does not rewrite the
  formula or re-sign the suite with identical contents. A tap whose history is full
  of no-op commits is one nobody will read during an incident.
- **A yanked or deleted release never gets promoted.** The soak window exists to
  give us a day to notice; the job must actually read the current state of the
  release list at promotion time, not a snapshot taken when the tag was cut.
- **The window is one stated number in one place**, and it agrees with whatever
  `channel.rs` still uses for non-package boxes. Two soak windows that can disagree
  is worse than either value.
- **Promotion is visible.** Something a person can read says which release is in
  nightly and when it got there — a line in the tap commit, a job summary, or a
  file in the repo. Today `lisa nightly status` answers this per box; after this
  ticket the answer exists centrally and should be readable without ssh.
- **Client-side soak is retired or scoped.** Say whether `DEFAULT_SOAK_HOURS` still
  governs curl-installed boxes, and if it does, say why two mechanisms is the right
  answer rather than an oversight.

## Notes

- This is the ticket that makes `brew install lisa-nightly` mean something more than
  `lisa-canary` with a different name. Without it the two formulae are identical and
  the fleet gains nothing.
- Worth deciding in review: what promotes a candidate to *stable*. Today it is a
  human dropping `-rc` from the workspace version, which is a real and deliberate
  act — automating that is out of scope here, but the promotion job is where someone
  will eventually want to.
- The failure mode to design against is a promotion job that quietly stops. A
  `lisa-nightly` frozen at a three-week-old release reads exactly like a healthy one
  from a machine's point of view.
