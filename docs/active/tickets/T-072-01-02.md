## Context

**Read `S-072-01` first.** `T-072-01-01` makes the spend readable. This is what
the desk does about it, and it is `phase: design` because the wrong answer here
is worse than no answer.

The failure the operator described:

> *"running out of session limit and all loops crash"*

Four panes across two machines discover the limit at the same moment, mid-ticket.
What is lost is not the allowance — that was going to be spent either way — it is
**the work in flight**, and the ledger rows that were about to be written.

The wanted behaviour, in their words:

> *"should orchestrate low priority lisa loop stoppage, proactively use sonnet 5,
> tweak effort per pane"*

Three different actions, at three different distances from the wall.

## The design question: what is allowed to act on its own

This is the ticket's real content. The desk has a rule that nothing runs when
nobody asked, and a rule that a tool refuses rather than guesses. A program that
notices the allowance is low and **downshifts four panes by itself** is doing
something the operator did not ask for at that moment — even if they asked for it
in general, a fortnight ago, in a config file.

Three positions, and one has to be chosen:

- **It only says.** The spend is on the desk line and in the inbox, and every
  action is the operator's. Safest, and it fails exactly when they are asleep —
  which is when the overnight loops are running.
- **It stops, but never starts or changes.** Running low ends the lowest-priority
  loops and does nothing else. Stopping is the reversible direction and loses
  only queued work; it cannot produce a surprising result the way a silent model
  swap can.
- **It downshifts.** Panes move to a cheaper model and lower effort and keep
  going. Gets the most work out of a thin allowance, and means a ticket can be
  finished by a model the operator did not choose for it — which the ledger
  will record, and which may be exactly wrong for the ticket in flight.

## Acceptance Criteria

- **The position above is chosen and written down**, with the reason. Whatever it
  is, it is a rule about autonomy and belongs in `docs/knowledge/`, not only in
  code.
- **Priority is a thing a board can have.** *"low priority lisa loop stoppage"*
  presumes some loop is lower priority than another, and nothing on this desk
  says which. Say where that is written and what it defaults to — and note that
  a board with no priority must not become the one that gets stopped.
- **Whatever acts, says it did, where the operator will see it.** A loop that
  stopped itself overnight is news for the morning inbox. `rail tell` is the
  road; a stopped loop is close to the `loop-degraded` fact that already exists.
- **Work in flight is ended cleanly, not killed.** The desk has spent two days on
  runs whose sessions outlived them and could not be put down —
  `S-070-01` is the whole story. Anything that stops a loop must leave it in a
  state `lisa schedulers` can describe and a person can restart.
- **Nothing acts on a number it could not read.** If a machine could not be
  asked, that is not a low reading.
- **The frontloading case stays legal.** Spending 80% on Monday is not an error.
  Whatever threshold exists is about *knowing*, not about pacing —
  `S-072-01` is explicit that this is not a rate limiter.
- Reproduce it: set the allowance low, run a board past it, and have the desk do
  the chosen thing and say so.

## Notes

- **The second failure mode is not solved here and should be said out loud.**
  *"using too much within a day, making the rest of the week unavailable"*
  cannot be caught at a threshold, because by the time it trips, the week is
  already spent. The only lever is the daily reading from `T-072-01-01` arriving
  in the morning brief, early enough to change what the day does. That is a
  reporting problem, not an orchestration one.
- The two gears the operator wants — *"an efficiency gear and a $400 a month
  double-quad-max-plan gear"* — are the same lever as this one pulled by hand.
  If the desk can downshift itself, it can also be told to, and that word is
  worth having whether or not the automatic version is built.
