## Context

**Read `S-067-01` first.**

`T-019-01` and `T-019-03` on `screen-design`, 2026-08-13. Each had **two attempt
directories with assignment files**, agents that worked for twelve minutes, a
reset, and a dead pane. The ledgers:

```
grep 'T-019-01' .lisa/provenance.jsonl         → nothing
grep 'T-019-01' .lisa/completion-journal.jsonl → nothing
```

**No row. Not a failure row, not a timeout row, nothing.** By the record, no
attempt on either ticket ever happened.

What was left instead was a state the board cannot explain:

```
T-019-01   review     *** NO SEAT ***
T-019-03   review     *** NO SEAT ***
```

Advanced to `review`, no seat holding them, no work published to
`docs/active/work/`, no commit naming them, and no ledger entry saying why. An
operator reading the board sees two tickets under way that nobody is working, with
nothing to read about it.

### Why this is worse than the pane dying

The pane dying is `T-067-01-01` and is fixable. **This is the part that makes it
invisible.** Every tool on the desk that answers *what happened here* reads these
files:

- `lisa status` reports the phase and cannot say a seat vanished
- `steer day` rolls up provenance and shows nothing for twelve minutes of work
- `rail desk` shows the board as busy, which is what the board says
- the run summary counts `failed: 0`

Two agent sessions were spent — real tokens, real wall clock — and the desk's own
accounting says the day contained neither.

## Acceptance Criteria

- **An attempt that was launched leaves a row**, whatever became of it. The
  assignment file is written before the agent starts; the ledger should not be
  able to disagree with the existence of that file.
- **A seat that is lost is recorded as lost**, with the reason. `timed-out` and
  `done` already exist; say what this one is called and make it distinguishable
  from both.
- **A ticket cannot sit in `implement` or `review` with no seat and no ledger
  entry.** Either the phase is walked back or the state is named. Today it is
  neither, and `reset-ticket` is the operator's only way out — the same hand
  repair `T-062-01-04` was written about.
- **The token spend is attributed.** `capture-usage` records a session's tokens
  from the Stop hook; a pane that dies before stopping loses them. Say whether
  those two sessions' usage is recoverable and, if not, that the loss is recorded
  rather than silent.
- **`run_summary` tells the truth.** `failed: 0` on a run that lost two seats is
  the summary being wrong in the direction that matters.
- Reproduce it: kill a pane mid-attempt and assert the ledger names the attempt
  and its end.

## Notes

- The write ordering is already understood in this codebase — `on-stop.sh` says
  *"Order matters: the stop signal is what lets the scheduler act on this pane …
  so the capture must already be durable when the signal appears — a session
  ended mid-capture lost 8 of 9 usage records."* The same reasoning applies at the
  other end of an attempt's life.
- Related to `S-060-01` and not the same: that is a lease nobody can release after
  a run died. This is an attempt with no evidence it existed, on a board whose run
  is still alive.
- **This is why the first diagnosis of `T-062-01-04` went unchallenged for a day.**
  A claim that three tickets "picked up and ran with no warning and no failure"
  could not be checked, because a board that reads normally is not evidence a run
  happened. The artifacts are the evidence, and here there were none to check.
