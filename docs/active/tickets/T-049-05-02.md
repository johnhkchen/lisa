## Context

The jargon-wall fix. The T-046-06-03 field block read: "The Codex closing leg measured 225 MiB against the ticket/story's approximately 200 MiB gate … John must either provide conforming reruns or explicitly amend both acceptance requirements before Review can pass." Technically correct, humanly unusable — a nontechnical operator meeting that as the first line of Waiting-on-you has no move to make, and that is the backlash dynamic: telling people to build with Lisa, then handing them a wall the first time a guard fires. Put a floor under every block, structured or not. (1) Rendering: every operator surface that shows a parked ticket (crates/lisa-cli/src/status.rs, crates/lisa-plugin/src/ui.rs) leads with one plain sentence — the ticket's plain ask when structured, or a standard line for legacy blocks: "This ticket needs a decision from you. The reviewer's note is below — you can paste it to your coding agent." — with the raw technical prose demoted below that line, never first. (2) Authoring: the review-phase instructions in docs/knowledge/rdspi-workflow.md (bundled copy kept in sync per templates.rs) require block asks written for a bystander — what to do, said plainly, jargon in the steps not the ask — with the T-046-06-03 text quoted as the counter-example. The reviewer can still be wrong or strict; that stays operator-appealable. This ticket only guarantees the human's first line is always actionable.

_Advances: no guard, however technical its reason, ever puts engineering prose in front of a human before a sentence they can act on — the E-048 promise held even when the reviewer breaks format._
