## Context

**Read `S-072-01` first.** This is the reading half. `T-072-01-02` is what to do
about it.

`.lisa/<client>/captures.jsonl` has one record per turn per pane, on every board,
and nothing has ever read one. Turning them into *how much of the week is gone*
needs three things the file does not have on its own.

## Acceptance Criteria

- **One command says what has been spent, over a window.** A week is the
  operator's unit and a day is the one that predicts it. Say both.
- **It counts by model, because the models are not the same price.**
  `T-071-01-02` is the dependency that makes this possible; until it lands, a
  sum over mixed models is a number that looks right and is not.
- **It counts the whole desk, not one board.** Four boards on two machines is
  one allowance. Use `rail desk --hosts --json` for who the machines are — that
  is the desk's one answer to that question — and read each machine's captures
  over the reach that already works.
- **A machine that cannot be asked is said, not treated as zero.** The rule this
  desk keeps relearning. An unreachable machine has almost certainly been
  spending.
- **It is honest about what it cannot know.** These are token counts from
  transcripts, not the provider's own accounting of an allowance. They will not
  match exactly, and the difference should be stated once, plainly, rather than
  discovered by someone trusting the number too far.
- **Records from before `T-071-01-02` are excluded or marked**, not silently
  folded in at today's model. The boundary is a fact.
- **It costs a file read.** No network, no provider API, no daemon. It is
  `jsonl` on disk plus one reach per remote machine.
- Reproduce it: run two boards on two models, ask what was spent, and have the
  answer break down by model and by machine.

## Notes

- **This is deliberately not a call to the provider.** No published API returns
  *"you have 35% of your week left"*, which is why the operator read it off a
  screen. What this desk can do is count its own spend and let a person calibrate
  it against what they see once. Say that in the output rather than implying an
  authority it does not have.
- Worth deciding in review: where the allowance itself is written down — a
  number in a config the operator sets, or nothing at all and the command reports
  raw totals. **The second is more honest and less useful**, and the choice
  decides whether `T-072-01-02` has a threshold to act on.
- `rail` should be able to print this on the desk line. That is a rail ticket,
  not this one, and it should read this command's answer rather than the ledger —
  two readers of one jsonl is two answers.
