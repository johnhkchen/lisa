## Intent — the bigger-picture play

The DAG view is the board at a glance — and at 49 live tickets the field board rendered wider than its pane, with lisa never noticing: the dashboard dispatch hands the DAG view no width at all (ui.rs ~1253; `render_dag_view`'s signature ~1288 never asks), `render_dag` emits full-width lines, and the terminal clips whatever doesn't fit. The deepest, most-connected rows — the ones you most need to see — are exactly the ones that fall off the table.

Look at what the ink buys. Every node spends two columns on `T-` (every ticket has one) and four on ` RDY` — a status word that color should carry, except the one place a status color is computed for the DAG it is immediately discarded (the post-processor destructures the pair and ignores the status half, ui.rs ~833), so the whole board renders status as unpainted text. That is the informationally repetitive detail worth shedding: node ceremony, not nodes. A good map degrades by dropping ornament first — `[T-015-04-02 RDY]` becomes `[15-04-02]` with status carried in color — and when even that spills, it says so in one honest line and lets you pan, rather than cropping in silence.

## Value to the design

Advances P4 — the whole board stays identifiable at a glance as it grows: a condensed node is six columns narrower, which buys back roughly a third of the board's width at field scale; status becomes a color scan instead of a read; and when the map still exceeds the table, the view says exactly how much is off-screen and which keys reach it. Silent clipping — the current behavior — is the P4 failure mode this epic retires.

## Done looks like

A 20-ticket board renders exactly as today, full labels. At 50 tickets the nodes read `[15-04-02]`, blocked ones scan red, working ones green, and the whole graph fits. At 80 tickets a single line under the header says the map is 37 columns wider than the pane and [h]/[l] pan across it — smoothly, colors intact, clamped at both edges. Nothing is ever cropped without the view saying so.

## Context & constraints

Layout belongs to ascii-dag 0.8 (crates/lisa-plugin/Cargo.toml ~19): `DAG::from_edges(&nodes, &edges).render()` (ui.rs ~812) owns layering, edge routing, and the `[ ]` bracket chrome. Lisa owns exactly two things — the label strings it passes in (`format!("{} {}", t.id, status_str)`, ui.rs ~789) and the ANSI colorization it applies afterward (~831–840) — and this epic operates only on those, plus measurement and panning around the result. Width arrives solely via `render(rows, cols)` (lib.rs ~8686) and must be threaded through to the DAG path — past the legacy `cols.min(100)` clamp renderers see today (ui.rs ~1669), which stays for the text presets but must not gate the DAG, or panes wider than 100 columns would condense boards that actually fit; the fit decision measures the widest **raw** rendered line, before color injection, so escape bytes never count as ink. Panning's named hazard: by slice time the lines carry injected ANSI, and a naive byte cut shears escape sequences into garbage — the slicer must count visible columns (slice raw and re-colorize, or an ANSI-aware cut; design decides). Done tickets stay filtered out as today (~752). Sequencing: paint the status color first (it is condensed mode's legibility budget), then the width-aware switch, then panning; the chain head waits on E-053's tail (shared ui.rs/lib.rs surface across the 0.4.5 UI train). NON-GOALS honored: N4 — no layout-engine replacement, no story-lane grouping, no subgraph collapsing; the epic varies labels, measures, and pans, nothing else. N3 — the overflow indicator reports what the render actually did, never a guess.

Decomposition artifact rule: children spell the constraints plainly — fit decisions are unit-tested at forced widths on synthetic boards; the ANSI-slicing hazard gets a fixture that walks every pan offset asserting intact escape sequences; "no silent cropping" is proven by a test where any clipped render must carry the indicator line.
