## Intent — the bigger-picture play

The doctrine this epic is named for is how executives survive delegation: staff never hand up a raw problem — they hand up a one-page memo with the situation in plain words, a recommendation, and a signature line. The executive's two moves are *sign* or *send back*. Both are cheap, both are recorded, and neither requires the executive to re-derive the staff work.

E-049 built lisa's andon cord — a block parks a ticket, level-triggered, no silent retirement — and the cord is right. What's missing is the memo. The field run showed the Operations screen greeting a person with codesign flags, `.appex` paths, and full criterion quotes — the exact tech-word-vomit lisa promised its operator they wouldn't need. Below it, an ATTENTION NEEDED box advertised "Press [d] to mark done" — and pressing [d] on those very tickets hits the fail-closed disposition guard and dies in a rejection modal. The one lever on the panel is painted on: a UI label substituting for a state transition that doesn't exist, which is N3's exact sin.

This epic builds both halves of the memo protocol. A **Present desk** — [p], one keypress from anywhere — lists each pending decision as a collapsed card: the ticket, how long it has waited, the one-sentence ask the disposition schema already mandates, and the recommended move. The paragraphs still exist, one keypress deep, for the operator who wants them. And **[d] becomes a signature that seals**: on a blocked or unreviewed ticket it offers a short catalog of canned dispositions — choices, not essays — and the chosen one rides the existing done-with-a-note machinery: work seals, dependents flow, the journal keeps the receipt. An operator who thinks the work is actually wrong doesn't sign — [s] sends it back for another review pass. Sign or send back. Nothing else to learn.

## Value to the design

Advances P4 — the supervising human reads a desk of three-line cards instead of paragraphs, and every card names its own next key; identifying what needs deciding takes a glance, deciding takes four keypresses. Advances P2 — the override is not a bypass: it produces a real disposition note, seals through the same completion path agents use, and leaves operator authority, the chosen reason, and the overridden ask in the provenance ledger and completion journal. The "Press [d]" hint stops promising what the code refuses; after this epic the advertised lever and the state machine agree.

## Done looks like

A ticket parks because no Apple ID is signed into Xcode on this machine. The desk shows: `T-015-02-02 · waiting 2h · Signed build needs an Apple ID in Xcode; the work itself checks out. → [d] accept as far as this machine can verify`. The operator presses [d], the recommended reason is preselected, Enter. The ticket seals, its dependents go ready on the next pass, Notes-for-you gains the receipt, and the journal shows who signed and why. On the next card the operator smells something off — [s] sends it back, and the board shows it re-entering review instead of haunting the attention box. At no point did anyone read a criterion quote they didn't ask for.

## Context & constraints

Grounds against crates/lisa-plugin/src/lib.rs — the [d] chain: `open_mark_done_modal` (~8253) → `mark_ticket_done` (~8308) → the operator guard in `dispatch_completion_at` (~2422) → `passing_review_disposition` (~1944), which reads `docs/active/work/<id>/review-disposition.json` fail-closed (missing/malformed ⇒ Invalid ⇒ rejection, crates/lisa-core/src/disposition.rs ~103). The lever to reuse, not rebuild: `ReviewDisposition::Note` already authorizes completion (disposition.rs ~98), and `finish_successful_completion` (~3109) already flips Done, rebuilds the DAG so dependents flow, emits the provenance note, and releases the seat — indifferent to completion source. The surfaces to fold: `render_waiting_on_you` / `render_notes_for_you` / `render_attention_banner` (crates/lisa-plugin/src/ui.rs ~460/~494/~522, static "Press [d]" hint ~704), fed by `collect_parked_remedies` (parking.rs ~121) and `collect_notes` (lib.rs ~8804). Key estate: [p] currently cycles presets (~8187); it becomes the desk, and the cycle moves to a free key ([v] suggested — research confirms against the chrome inventory). The MarkDone modal has no text input (~8150–8182) and gains none: canned reasons only.

Discipline carried over from S-049-06: a note is a criteria-vs-evidence instrument with real citations — the operator catalog maps onto that schema (the citation names what the operator actually looked at: the block's own disposition, review.md, progress.md; for the fail-closed shapes with no block to quote, the quote records the literal state being overridden and the citation the inspected path), and no catalog entry may read as a quality hedge; doubting the work routes to send-back, never to a note. NON-GOALS honored: N2 — an override ends in a named, journaled state, and send-back re-enters the bounded review cycle, not a retry loop. N3 — no UI string may advertise a transition the state machine refuses; the current hint is the type specimen and its correction is acceptance-tested. N4 — the completion, parking, and journal machinery is reused as-is; this epic adds one authority branch and one view, not a second completion system. Sequencing: the override path lands first (it is the desk's live ammunition), the modal second, the desk view third, its keys last — each ticket rides the one before it; the chain head waits on E-052's tail (shared lib.rs/ui.rs surface, and the desk's ages use E-052's formatter). An automated first-responder that triages blocks before they reach the human is deliberately NOT in this epic — it composes on top of the desk later.

Decomposition artifact rule: children spell the constraints plainly — every card-copy claim carries a kitchen-table check in review.md (no mechanism words on collapsed cards); the no-bypass claim is proven by a negative fixture showing a Blocked ticket still refuses to seal without an operator-selected reason; receipts are shown end-to-end (ledger line, journal entry, Notes-for-you card) in progress notes.
