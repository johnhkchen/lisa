## Context

Before any copy or layout work starts, we need one clear, agreed statement of
who this page is for and what the single "bottom line" message is — the one
sentence a non-technical, brand-new visitor should walk away with even if
they read nothing else.

This ticket is research and decision-making, not page-building. It defines:

- Who the visitor is (what they're trying to build, what they already tried,
  what they're worried about) for someone who is not deeply technical.
- The single bottom-line value proposition, stated in plain, jargon-free
  language.
- The 2-4 supporting reasons to believe it (trust, control, not babysitting
  every step, not losing work).
- What NOT to lead with (protocol names, workflow phases, git internals,
  DAGs, Zellij) and why those belong further down or off this page entirely.

## Acceptance Criteria

- A short written brief (audience description + bottom-line message +
  supporting reasons + explicit non-goals for messaging) exists and is
  referenced by later tickets.
- The bottom-line message is one sentence, contains no jargon a
  non-technical reader would have to look up, and is specific to the
  visitor's actual goal (building their project) rather than describing
  Lisa's internals.
- The brief explicitly lists which technical details (protocol/workflow
  names, DAG/scheduling internals, git sealing mechanics) are deferred to
  later on the page or out of scope, so later copy tickets don't
  reintroduce them in the opening pitch.
